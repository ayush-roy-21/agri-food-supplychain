India's total exports reached a record USD 863 billion in FY2025-26, nearly doubling from USD 468 billion in FY2014-15, reflecting sustained momentum in both goods and services trade over the past decade. The figures were shared by Nitin Kumar Yadav, Additional Secretary at the Union Ministry of Commerce, during a convention of exporters, industry bodies and farmer producer organisations in Chandigarh on Tuesday. According to Yadav, India's exports expanded at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 5.7 per cent over the period, supported by robust growth in services exports and rising diversification of the country's export basket. "During this period, merchandise exports grew from USD 310 billion to USD 442 billion, services exports surged from USD 158 billion to USD 421 billion at a CAGR of 9.3 per cent, and non-petroleum exports reached a new record of USD 387.9 billion, underscoring the depth and diversity of India's export base," he said. The sharp rise in services exports emerged as a key driver of overall trade growth, accounting for nearly half of India's total exports in FY26. Services shipments increased by more than 166 per cent over the decade, highlighting India's growing strength in sectors such as information technology, business services and professional consulting. Trade Agreements Strengthen Global Market AccessYadav said India has strengthened its position in global trade through a series of strategic free trade agreements aimed at improving market access and integrating the country into global value chains. Among the key agreements is the India-European Free Trade Association (EFTA) Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement (TEPA), which came into effect in 2025. The pact is backed by a proposed USD 100 billion foreign direct investment commitment and provides duty-free access for around 98 per cent of India's exports to EFTA member countries.
