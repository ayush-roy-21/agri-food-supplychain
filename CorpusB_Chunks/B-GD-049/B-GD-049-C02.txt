Wheat stocks stood at 53.41m tonnes on June 1, well above the government’s target of 27.6m tonnes and the highest since 2021, the data showed. “Rice stocks are more than adequate. That should give the government the confidence to continue exports despite forecasts of below-normal rainfall, which could affect production,” said a New Delhi-based dealer at a global trading firm. India, which accounts for around 40pc of global rice exports, removed the last of its export curbs on the grain in March 2025. In recent years, India has held large rice stocks while facing tight wheat supplies, but this year the country has abundant inventories of both grains, said a Mumbai-based dealer. “The government surprised the market by procuring 35m tonnes of wheat. It is now in a comfortable position and can release stocks aggressively into the market to keep prices in check,” the dealer said. India’s rice and wheat production reached record highs in the 2025/26 crop year, at 154.02m tonnes and 120.66m tonnes, respectively, as ample monsoon rainfall encouraged farmers to expand acreage. ================================================================================
