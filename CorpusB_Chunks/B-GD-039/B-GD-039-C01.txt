The rice industry in West Bengal has intensified its appeal to the newly formed state administration, urging faster reforms in infrastructure development, streamlined regulatory approvals, and stronger export promotion strategies to unlock investment potential and long-term sectoral growth. Industry representatives argue that while the state remains one of India’s most important agricultural hubs, persistent gaps in rural connectivity, logistics support, and administrative efficiency continue to slow down expansion in rice milling, storage, and export-linked activities, particularly in production-heavy rural districts. Ricevilla Foods CEO Suraj Agarwal pointed out that inadequate rural road networks, weak drainage systems, and delayed electricity connections are among the biggest barriers affecting the establishment of modern rice mills and warehousing units. He explained that many entrepreneurs in interior districts face prolonged waiting periods for electricity approvals, sometimes stretching into several months, which significantly increases project costs and discourages new investment. According to him, improving basic infrastructure and ensuring faster utility connections would directly enhance efficiency across the rice production value chain and make the sector more competitive in both domestic and global markets.
