Industry stakeholders are also pressing for the introduction of a single-window clearance system to simplify licensing procedures, reduce bureaucratic delays, and accelerate the setup of new milling and processing facilities, which they believe is critical for sustaining growth momentum. In addition, the industry has proposed the establishment of a dedicated Rice Industry Development Board that would bring together farmers, mill operators, exporters, and government agencies under a coordinated framework. This demand comes at a time when India’s rice exports have surged nearly 19 percent in 2025, reaching approximately 215 lakh tonnes following the easing of earlier export restrictions. India now dominates global rice trade, contributing close to 40 percent of worldwide exports and shipping more than 200 lakh tonnes annually out of an estimated 550 lakh tonnes global market. Exporters argue that improving rail connectivity and expanding port infrastructure in eastern India would significantly strengthen export competitiveness and reduce logistics costs. Premium rice varieties from West Bengal, including Gobindo Bhog and Tulai Panji, are also witnessing increasing demand in both domestic and international markets due to their unique aroma and cultural significance. Industry voices suggest that targeted branding and global promotional campaigns could elevate their recognition and export value. Meanwhile, the West Bengal Rice Mills Owners Association has raised concerns over the impact of extensive free rice distribution under welfare schemes, arguing that large-scale coverage under both state and central programs is weakening market demand. They estimate that around 1,500 rice mills operate in the state, but many are either struggling or shut due to low profitability. Stakeholders believe that a more balanced policy approach, combined with stronger export focus, could help the state capture a larger share of India’s Rs 90,000 crore rice export industry.
