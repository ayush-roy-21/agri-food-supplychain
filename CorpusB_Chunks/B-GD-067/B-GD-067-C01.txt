The Indian Tea Development Corporation has enforced a new rule requiring laboratory testing (lab tests) for every truckload of imported tea, causing a complete halt in Nepali tea exports for the past two weeks. Businesspeople say that this policy tightening by India has pushed Nepal’s tea industry—which earns around NPR 5 to 6 billion in foreign currency annually—into a major crisis. According to Aditya Parajuli, president of the Nepal Tea Producers Association, India introduced a new “Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)” effective from Baisakh 18. The new provision has increased administrative and financial burdens for Nepali exporters. “This is the peak season for tea exports. Around 500,000 kilograms of tea are currently ready for export,” said President Parajuli. “However, because of the Indian side’s new and stricter provisions, no trader has dared to send tea to India. Not even a single packet of tea has been exported in the past two weeks.” Previously, once a Nepali tea factory passed a laboratory test, it could export up to 10 truckloads of tea to India within 15 days using the same certificate. But under the new rule, as soon as tea trucks enter the Panitanki customs point from the Kakarbhitta border, samples from every truck must compulsorily be sent to a laboratory recognized by the Indian government. A fee of NPR 18,000 must be paid for testing each truck sample, and the report is said to take at least 14 days to arrive. Indian laboratories will upload the report to the online system within 14 days, and if the test fails, the tea will not only be barred from sale in the Indian market or export to third countries, but may also be confiscated under the strict new provision.
