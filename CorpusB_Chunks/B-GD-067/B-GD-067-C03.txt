The Nepal Tea Producers Association has demanded that the Nepal government immediately begin government-to-government (G2G) dialogue with the Indian side to remove this non-tariff barrier in the tea sector. The association says efforts are underway. Nepal-produced CTC and orthodox tea are considered popular in the global market for their quality. However, policy barriers in India—the main market—have put the livelihoods of everyone involved, from farmers to large industrialists and hundreds of thousands of dependent workers, at risk. According to statistics from the National Tea and Coffee Development Board, in fiscal year 2081/82, Nepal produced 26.123 million kilograms of processed tea from tea plantations spread across 20,602 hectares of land. During the same fiscal year, Nepal exported tea worth NPR 4.59 billion. Across the country, there are 170 large tea estates and 120 small and medium-sized tea gardens. More than 15,000 small farmers are directly involved in tea production, while around 100,000 workers are directly employed from plantations to factories.
