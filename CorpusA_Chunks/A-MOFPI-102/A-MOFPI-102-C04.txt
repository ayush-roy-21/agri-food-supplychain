2.1. Introduction to Food Supply chain 
A robust food supply chain is essential in order to produce safe food products that meet the 
consumer demand for high quality food. The retail and hospitality industries that buy the 
products, for example, want to purchase high quality food at a low price from the supplier so 
they can still make a profit and offer competitive prices. The key to meeting consumer 
demand for high quality food products starts with a well-managed food supply chain, and this 
will also help to deter problems from arising and causing loss down the line. 
Food supply chains are also vital in ensuring  food safety  and traceability of products, 
meaning both manufacturers and consumers can easily trace food back to i ts origins. For 
manufacturers, this is needed if product recalls or withdrawals are required and for 
consumers, it means that they can understand the origin of their food and how it has been 
produced.  
In recent years, people have become more aware of the environmental impact that certain 
food supply chains may be having. A key takeaway from this is the focus on short food 
supply chains. There are economic, environmental and social benefits to shorter food chains 
and the increased connection to where their food comes from, is likely to lead to less waste 
and increased trust among consumers. 
Longer supply chains, on the other hand, result in less understanding of the agricultural 
processes behind the food we eat, the challenges faced by farmers and the impact  of our 
choices on the environment. In fact, short food chains offer mutual benefits to both farmers 
and consumers, acting as a model to increase transparency, trust, growth and equity. 
What is Food Supply Chain? 
The food supply chain is a summary of every  process involved in producing and delivering 
food to households and distributors. Food goes through a lot of processes before landing on a 
person’s table. All of these processes from farming, processing and packaging, and down to 
food delivery are a part of the food supply chain.

Why Food Supply Chain is Important? 
Now days, Food supply chain holds a lot of significance by having a proper chain wherein 
farmers, food processors and distributors can trace each process which allows more 
transparency to them so that the event of small contamination source can easily traceable an d 
finding solutions is much simpler.

traceable.

How It Works? 
A food supply chain or network is an end -to-end logistics integration that regulates the food 
supply’s working and assures robust management. The food supply chain network begins 
from the input supplier integrating a chain for end -to-end fulfilment. The input supplier 
sources the commodities for the integration through inventory management, transport, and 
storage, followed by distribution to the  end-users. The network of the food supply chain 
differs from the cold chain management, as the requirement varies for storage, transport, and 
distribution. However, several channels play an integral role in determining the syst em’s 
efficiency apart from that the forecasting, planning, and consumer behaviour. These channels 
significantly impact the supply chain’s working dynamics and work on the newly introduced 
concept of valued integration of supply chains. According to this concept, the supply chain 
management companies in India understand the system from the perspective of the end -user, 
analyse from the demand -pull approach, and consistently make an effort to encourage 
innovation and development in the food supply chain and networking.  It is essential to  
understand the working dynamics of the food supply networks while entering the business 
and arranging a fully functional end -to-end supply chain integration; it is also important to 
source the arrangement from top supply chain companies in India.
