Betweenness centrality
The concept of betweenness is a measure of how often a node is located on the shortest path between
other nodes in the network. Those nodes positioned on the shortest path between other nodes play the
roles of mediator that influences any two nodes without direct contact. Nodes with higher betweenness
centrality are those positioned at the core of the network:

The closeness centrality of a node is defined by the inverse of the average length of the shortest paths
to/from all the other nodes in the network. Higher closeness centrality indicates higher influence on

Eigenvector centrality measures
Consider an undirected network represented by a graph G ¼ (V, E), where V denotes the set of nodes
(1, 2, …,c , …,d , …, v) and E the set of edges. Let A be the co-occurrence matrix for this graph and
its element acd ¼ 1 (or edge weightage in case of weighted network) if nodes c and d are connected by
an edge and acd ¼ 0 if they are not.
