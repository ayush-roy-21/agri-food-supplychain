The historical relations of the research themes emerged during the periods 2002– 2014 and
2002– 2018, as shown in Figure 7. The broader thematic context is obtained for the period
2002– 2014, whereas the inclusion of recent articles (i.e. 2015– 2018) resulted in identifying the
emerging research directions out of these broader issues. Through this approach, we are able
to identify the dynamic evolution of the research domain. The first cluster “Production and
distribution in AFN” has emerged from the Cluster 2 of period 2002– 14, as shown in Figure 7.
In recent years, AFN research is focussed on online spaces for reconnection (Bos and Owen,
2016), consumer behaviour towards buying in short food supply chains (SFSC) (Giampietri
et al., 2016a), trust and relationship in AFN (Roy et al., 2017; Pinna, 2017), farmer ’sm a r k e t
(Schupp, 2017), economic sharing in AFN (Miralles et al., 2017) and so on.
The second cluster “Relationship, safety, and standards in the food supply chain ” has
emerged from Cluster 3 of 2002 – 2014, which focussed on improving the performance of the
supply chain through the relationship among members. Recent articles in this sub-theme
are focussed on retailers ’ adoption of safety standards and its impact on organizations
sourcing methodology. It is observed that the publications on the contractual relationship
among supply chain members from the first cluster (market economics of contract farming
in the FSC) during 2002 – 2014 are clustered with this subgroup (e.g. Reardon et al. , 2014;
Poulton et al. , 2010).
