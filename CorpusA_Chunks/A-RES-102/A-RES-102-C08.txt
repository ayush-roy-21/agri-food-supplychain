Digital transformation can help identify inefficiencies in 
the food supply chain and facilitate the redistribution of 
surplus food to those in need. Technologies such as AI 
and machine learning can also predict and prevent food 
spoilage, reducing waste at all stages of the agri-food 
system [17] . AI enhances decision-making in agri-food 
systems by analyzing vast amounts of data from sensors, 
satellites, and market trends to guide farmers, pro -
cessors, and supply chain managers. Moreover, AI-driven 
predictive analytics uses historical data and machine 
learning models to foresee and mitigate risks in agri-food 
systems. AI-powered automation can transform a system 
from static setting to dynamic and self-learning; it can 
reduce labor dependency, increase efficiency, and 
minimize natural resources and waste throughout the 
agri-food value chain. Digital platforms can facilitate the 
exchange of by-products and waste materials between 
different actors in the agri-food system, promoting cir -
cular economy practices such as recycling, composting, 
and energy recovery. This can help reduce the en -
vironmental impact of food production and create new 
revenue streams for farmers and businesses [18] .
Digital transformation in the agri-food system
The agri-food sector is experiencing major transforma -
tions due to factors like the rapid growth of the global 
population, climate change, and economic globalization 
[5–7] . Contemporary terms such as Agriculture 4.0, 
Precision Agriculture, and Smart farming all refer to 
various forms of DT adoptions in this sector [5,19] . 
Therefore, to promote digital transformations in this 
field, it is crucial to understand the characteristics of the 
agri-food supply chain, like the perishable nature of its 
products, emphasis on customer satisfaction, and en -
vironmental impact [20] . The agri-food supply chain 
entails significant challenges impacting the entire chain, 
including sustainability issues like high energy and 
water consumption, land use and soil erosion, in -
adequate waste management, and greenhouse gas 
emissions [21] . Additionally, there are challenges related 
to food waste stemming from transportation, insufficient 
storage facilities, stringent export regulations, food 
safety and security such as food fraud, traceability dis -
crepancies, and inadequate implementation of food se -
curity initiatives [22] . Table 1 represents different 
elements of the agri-food’s value chain, such as 
production, processing, distribution/consumption, and 
the disposal/reusing system with the impact of DTs and 
their application in agri-food systems.
Drivers of digital transformation in agri-food 
system
Digital transformation is a prerequisite and enabler for 
improving products and services, as well as the man -
agement of operations, making them more efficient. It 
also offers the potential for cost reduction and a com -
petitive advantage in the market. The key drivers of 
digital transformation include technological advance -
ments (for more examples, see Table 1 , component of 
DT), increasing demand for efficiency and productivity, 
food safety and traceability, sustainability, resource 
management, changing market dynamics and competi -
tiveness, connectivity, and infrastructure development 
(for more examples, see Table 1 , impact of DT).
The agri-food industry is confronted with various market 
challenges, including the unpredictable nature of com -
modity prices, the ever-evolving consumer preferences, 
and the complex landscape of global trade regulations 
[20] . However, with the advent of digital transformation, 
businesses can now effectively navigate these challenges 
through the strategic use of data and insights [35] . DTs 
enable enterprises to swiftly adapt to market changes, 
make well-informed decisions, and offer personalized 
products and services to their customers [36] . This en -
ables businesses within the agri-food industry to remain 
competitive in today’s dynamic marketplace.
The ever-growing accessibility of internet connectivity, 
especially in rural regions, combined with the advance -
ment in digital infrastructure, has opened up a world of 
possibilities for the digitization of agri-food systems. 
Farmers, agribusinesses, and various stakeholders can 
now tap into a wealth of valuable information, effort -
lessly communicate and collaborate, establish market 
connections, and harness the power of digital tools and 
platforms to enhance their operations.
The digital transformation of the agri-food sector is in -
tertwined with geopolitical factors, necessitating adap -
tive strategies for resilience. Cross-border data flows and 
e-commerce, crucial to this transformation, are influ -
enced by trade policies and regulations. Geopolitical 
events like trade wars and regional conflicts can disrupt 
supply chains, prompting the sector to leverage tech -
nologies such as IoT, blockchain, AI, and modeling for 
agility and resilience.
