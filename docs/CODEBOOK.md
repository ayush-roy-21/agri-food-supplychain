# CODEBOOK: The Barrier Horizon
**Sustainable Supplier Hurdles Facing Indian Agri-Food MSMEs**

This codebook dictates the canonical qualitative taxonomy governing Corpus A and Corpus B. It outlines the structural mapping rules applied to raw documents during Stage 4 metadata enrichment prior to vector embedding. 

---

## 1. The Four Loci (Thematic Scopes)
A locus in this study captures the fundamental nature of the hurdle facing the exporter. Every substantive document must be mapped to one of these four primary loci, or tagged as `inductive-other`/`not-applicable`.

### 1.1 Internal Capability and Resources (`internal-capability`)
**Definition**: The firm lacks the money, skills, laboratories, or scale required to meet compliance demands. This locus captures hurdles rooted inside the boundaries of the firm, such as the inability to afford fixed certification fees on thin margins, lack of technical training, or insufficient minimum-volume to justify compliance overhead.
**Mapping Terms**: `['cost', 'fee', 'expensive', 'lack', 'capacity', 'skill', 'scale', 'margin', 'afford', 'infrastructure']`

### 1.2 Relational Power and Dependence (`relational-power`)
**Definition**: Buyers, agents, or lead firms hold the power in the value chain. This locus captures asymmetric friction where the exporter is at the mercy of intermediary demands, consolidated buyer requirements, or unequal contract terms that pass the burden of compliance downward.
**Mapping Terms**: `['buyer', 'agent', 'power', 'intermediary', 'monopoly', 'lead firm', 'dependence', 'contract', 'leverage']`

### 1.3 Institutional Voids and Legitimacy (`institutional-voids`)
**Definition**: The rules environment is fragmented, uncertain, or the exporter carries origin stigma. This locus captures friction generated by the state or the market's structure, including contradictory domestic vs. destination standards, unpredictable enforcement, or blanket stigma applied to Indian origins.
**Mapping Terms**: `['fragmentation', 'uncertainty', 'stigma', 'ambiguous', 'unclear', 'overlap', 'contradiction', 'reputation', 'bias']`

### 1.4 Informational Verifiability (`informational-verifiability`)
**Definition**: Proof of sustainability is costly, traceability breaks, or certificates are not believed. This locus captures the friction of proving compliance, where the physical product might be sound, but the data trail (geotagging, digital certificates, supply chain visibility) is broken or rejected by destination authorities.
**Mapping Terms**: `['proof', 'certificate', 'traceability', 'unverifiable', 'documentation', 'data', 'geotag', 'record', 'blockchain', 'digital']`

## 2. The Five Verification Logics
Verification logic categorizes the property-space logic of the institutional compliance test or the operational hurdle encountered.

### 2.1 Residue and MRL Testing (`residue-and-mrl`)
**Definition**: Mandatory analytical testing required to prove compliance with Maximum Residue Limits (MRLs), microbiological thresholds, and pathogen absence.
**Mapping Terms**: `['testing', 'assay', 'screening', 'laboratory', 'hplc', 'lc-ms-ms', 'gc-ms-ms', 'mrl', 'nabl', 'sampling', 'pathogen', 'residue', 'aflatoxin', 'eto', 'salmonella']`

### 2.2 Catch Legality and Aquaculture Residue (`catch-legality-aquaculture`)
**Definition**: Mechanisms enforcing marine health, IUU (Illegal, Unreported, and Unregulated) fishing prevention, and pre-harvest aquaculture controls.
**Mapping Terms**: `['marine', 'aquaculture', 'catch', 'iuu', 'chloramphenicol', 'nitrofuran', 'pht', 'catch-certificate']`

### 2.3 Land-Use Geolocation (`land-use-geolocation`)
**Definition**: Farm-level polygon mapping and digital geotagging systems required to prove product origin and zero-deforestation mandates (e.g., EUDR).
**Mapping Terms**: `['trace', 'tracenet', 'geotagg', 'polygon', 'deforestation', 'eudr', 'land-use', 'farm-mapping']`

### 2.4 Facility and Process Certification (`facility-and-process`)
**Definition**: Pre-requisite hygiene standards, internal capability audits, and processing plant approvals (e.g., HACCP, GFSI, ISO-17025).
**Mapping Terms**: `['audit', 'hygiene', 'haccp', 'iso-17025', 'facility', 'plant', 'packhouse', 'gfsi', 'inspection']`

### 2.5 Not Applicable (`not-applicable`)
**Definition**: Used for documents (like background literature or macro-level trade discourse) that do not describe a specific verification logic.
**Mapping Terms**: Applies automatically when documents do not match the specific mapping terms of the active logics.

## 3. Actor Vocabulary
Every document is tagged with the primary actor framing the hurdle. The allowed vocabulary is strictly limited to the following seven types:

1. **`regulator`**: State or institutional bodies (e.g., APEDA, FDA, EU DG SANTE).
2. **`buyer`**: International importers, retail brands, or lead firms purchasing the commodity.
3. **`certifier`**: Third-party auditors, private certification bodies, and accredited laboratories.
4. **`intermediary`**: Trade agents, brokers, and supply chain middlemen.
5. **`firm`**: The MSME exporter, processor, or manufacturer themselves.
6. **`ngo`**: Civil society organizations, advocacy groups, or environmental watchdogs.
7. **`media`**: Journalistic entities, trade publications, or public discourse forums.

## 4. Term-List Mapping Priority Rules
When executing automated classification (e.g., via `enrich_metadata_4x5.py`), the parsing engine evaluates strings based on the following prioritization:
1. Residue and MRL Testing
2. Catch Legality and Aquaculture Residue
3. Land-Use Geolocation
4. Facility and Process Certification
5. Not Applicable (Fallback)
