# The Object Anchor Test

The object of this study, stated as a test:

The study is about one thing: the hurdles Indian agri-food MSMEs face in being recognised as sustainable suppliers, so that they can enter and stay in global agricultural supply chains. Every unit of data, every topic, and every coded hurdle must pass a three-part anchor test, and anything failing any part is out of the analysis.

## Part One: The Actor Test
The unit concerns MSME-scale export operations, established by the scale ladder (Tier 1 register-verified, Tier 2 strongly indicated, Tier 3 weakly indicated) or by **hurdle-scale coupling**, meaning the hurdle's own economics only bind at small scale, such as fixed certification fees against thin margins or per-consignment testing costs.

## Part Two: The Recognition Test
The unit concerns being accepted, certified, verified, approved, listed, or retained as a supplier, or the failure of these, meaning rejection, delisting, carding, border refusal, certificate non-acceptance, or audit failure. General trade news, export volume statistics, ministerial aspiration, and policy commentary fail this part unless they contain a recognition event or requirement.

## Part Three: The Chain Test
The unit concerns entry into or standing within a global chain, meaning EU or US market access, buyer requirements, import controls, or due-diligence demands transmitted down the chain. Domestic-only matters fail unless they are the domestic leg of an export requirement.

***

**Application Rule:** A hurdle exists in this study only where all three parts pass. The test is conjunctive, the default is exclusion, and no unit is retained because it is interesting, voluminous, or expensive to have collected.
