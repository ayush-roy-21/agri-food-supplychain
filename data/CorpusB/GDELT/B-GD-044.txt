India's total exports reached a record USD 863 billion in FY2025-26, nearly doubling from USD 468 billion in FY2014-15, reflecting sustained momentum in both goods and services trade over the past decade.

The figures were shared by Nitin Kumar Yadav, Additional Secretary at the Union Ministry of Commerce, during a convention of exporters, industry bodies and farmer producer organisations in Chandigarh on Tuesday.

According to Yadav, India's exports expanded at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 5.7 per cent over the period, supported by robust growth in services exports and rising diversification of the country's export basket.

"During this period, merchandise exports grew from USD 310 billion to USD 442 billion, services exports surged from USD 158 billion to USD 421 billion at a CAGR of 9.3 per cent, and non-petroleum exports reached a new record of USD 387.9 billion, underscoring the depth and diversity of India's export base," he said.

The sharp rise in services exports emerged as a key driver of overall trade growth, accounting for nearly half of India's total exports in FY26. Services shipments increased by more than 166 per cent over the decade, highlighting India's growing strength in sectors such as information technology, business services and professional consulting.

Trade Agreements Strengthen Global Market AccessYadav said India has strengthened its position in global trade through a series of strategic free trade agreements aimed at improving market access and integrating the country into global value chains.

Among the key agreements is the India-European Free Trade Association (EFTA) Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement (TEPA), which came into effect in 2025. The pact is backed by a proposed USD 100 billion foreign direct investment commitment and provides duty-free access for around 98 per cent of India's exports to EFTA member countries.

He also highlighted the India-European Union Free Trade Agreement signed in 2026, which covers 27 high-income economies and is expected to support exports worth approximately Rs 3.2 lakh crore through tariff concessions on 99 per cent of bilateral trade by value.

In addition, the India-Mauritius Comprehensive Economic Cooperation and Partnership Agreement (CECPA) and the India-UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) provide zero-duty access on 99 per cent of India's exports to those markets. Bilateral trade between India and the UAE has already crossed USD 80 billion, Yadav noted.

Opportunities for Punjab and Haryana ExportersThe commerce ministry official said northern states stand to benefit significantly from India's expanding trade footprint.

For Punjab, major export opportunities lie in textiles and apparel, engineering products, agriculture and food processing sectors. Haryana, meanwhile, has emerged as a key exporter of Basmati rice, buffalo meat, non-Basmati rice, natural honey, dairy products and processed foods, while pharmaceuticals and chemicals offer strong growth potential going forward.

The event also marked the inauguration of a new regional office of the Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority (APEDA) in Chandigarh, aimed at supporting exporters and agri-businesses across the region.

The latest export figures underscore India's growing importance in global trade, with policymakers increasingly banking on services, value-added manufacturing and trade partnerships to sustain export-led growth in the coming years.
