Malt Indian Whisky, 3rd Place, 89.70 points Tasting Notes: Chocolate, vanilla, spice, dried apple, apricot, fruit jam, peat dust, banana, toffee, melon, salt Find Your Next Bottle: £45 / $70 Agneya, whose name comes from the Sanskrit word for “belonging to fire,” is Indri’s lightly peated single malt and a permanent part of the range rather than a limited run, which sets it apart from the two bottles above. It was matured in a combination of ex-bourbon and Pedro Ximénez sherry casks and bottled at 46% ABV, without chill filtration or added color. As with the rest of the range, it comes from Piccadily’s distillery in Indri, Haryana, and is made from indigenous six-row Indian barley. Of the three Indri whiskies here, Agneya is the easiest to find abroad, and it has sold across a number of export markets. It does, however, seem difficult to find in India. Award: Best Blended Indian Whisky, 1st Place, 85.87 points Tasting Notes: Fruit, vanilla, coconut, butterscotch, caramel, oak, nuts, spice The three blended winners work differently from the Indri single malts above them. Rather than being distilled entirely from malted barley at one distillery, they are Indian blended whiskies that combine imported Scotch malt with Indian grain spirit, a style that reflects how a large share of the Indian whisky market is built. Blender’s Pride Reserve Collection sits at the top of that group. Pernod Ricard launched it in December 2011, and at the time it was the most expensive whisky produced in India.
