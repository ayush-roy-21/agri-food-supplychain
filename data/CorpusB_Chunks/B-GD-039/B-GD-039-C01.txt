Synopsis West Bengal’s rice industry is urging the government to improve infrastructure and speed up regulatory clearances to strengthen production, storage, and export capacity. Industry stakeholders say better logistics, modern milling facilities, and faster approvals are essential to improve competitiveness and reduce supply chain bottlenecks. The rice industry in West Bengal has intensified its appeal to the newly formed state administration, urging faster reforms in infrastructure development, streamlined regulatory approvals, and stronger export promotion strategies to unlock investment potential and long-term sectoral growth. Industry representatives argue that while the state remains one of India’s most important agricultural hubs, persistent gaps in rural connectivity, logistics support, and administrative efficiency continue to slow down expansion in rice milling, storage, and export-linked activities, particularly in production-heavy rural districts. Ricevilla Foods CEO Suraj Agarwal pointed out that inadequate rural road networks, weak drainage systems, and delayed electricity connections are among the biggest barriers affecting the establishment of modern rice mills and warehousing units. He explained that many entrepreneurs in interior districts face prolonged waiting periods for electricity approvals, sometimes stretching into several months, which significantly increases project costs and discourages new investment. According to him, improving basic infrastructure and ensuring faster utility connections would directly enhance efficiency across the rice production value chain and make the sector more competitive in both domestic and global markets. Agarwal further emphasized that the scale of operations has increased substantially as India’s rice output has crossed 1,500 lakh tonnes annually, while government-held stocks have risen to over 590 lakh tonnes. He noted that efficient logistics, scientific storage systems, and modern transport infrastructure are now essential to prevent post-harvest losses and maintain grain quality over time.
