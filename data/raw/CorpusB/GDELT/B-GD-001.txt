FULL SUBSTANTIVE ARTICLE TEXT:
Indian spice exporters, particularly small and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs), are experiencing severe supply chain disruptions following European Union (EU) Directorate-General for Health and Food Safety (DG SANTE) border alerts regarding ethylene oxide (EtO) contamination. Several consignments of chilli powder, cumin, and cardamom have been detained at Rotterdam and Hamburg ports. The Spices Board of India has mandated pre-shipment sampling and analytical testing at quality evaluation laboratories (QELs), adding between 10 to 14 days to export lead times. Exporters note that the cost of multi-residue pesticide screening and ethylene oxide quantification is eroding profit margins for smaller farmers and aggregators. Furthermore, European buyers are increasingly demanding third-party ISO/IEC 17025 accredited laboratory certificates before releasing payment under Letters of Credit.
