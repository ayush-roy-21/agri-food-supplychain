2.4 Text mining (TM)
To find out the research focus of each sub-field, the contents of either all or the lead papers
in each sub-field are analysed and evaluated. The interpretation and analysis of sub-field
depend greatly on subjective factors and require expertise in the specific research field. To
avoid the reviewer bias and for a deeper understanding of the concepts addressed in each
sub-field, researchers have further used text mining (TM) as a content analysis technique to
retrieve the co-occurred words from the documents and to build a conceptual structure of
the domain (Ghadge et al., 2012; Song and Chambers, 2014). In this research, the co-occurred
words from the research articles in each sub-field are retrieved using TM to identify
research concept precisely. Co-word analysis presumes that a group of aggregated terms/
words could indicate the underlying themes and that co-occurrences of terms/words could
show the associations with the underlying themes. Title and abstract of all articles in each
sub-field are downloaded from the WoS database in .txt format and text mined separately
using Standford NLP core 3.6.0. StanfordcoreNLP 3.6.0 software is used in this research for
TM since it is flexible and simple (Song and Chambers, 2014).
Using Stanford metrics conversion Handler Programme, the output extensible markup
language file from the Standford NLP is converted to a GraphML network file from the
identified co-occurred words. The GraphML file is then used as input in Gephi for visualization
and further analysis of the co-word network. After establishing a co-word network, the
research concept of each cluster is then identified by measuring the relative frequency of these
co-occurred words and by measuring the network properties based on node and network-level
metrics (Barbara and Wichmann, 2016). The node-level metrics can be divided into local:
degree centrality, and the ones relative to the rest of the network (i.e. based on node’s location
within a network): closeness, betweenness and eigenvector centrality (Milojevic, 2013). The
number of direct links between a node and other nodes in the cluster is defined by degree
centrality. A word with a higher degree will be in the centre of the network and considered as
the information hub. The closeness centrality is defined by the inverse of the average length of
the shortest paths to or from all the other nodes in the network. Higher closeness centrality
refers to higher influence on other nodes. The betweenness centrality is a measure of how often
a node is located on the shortest path between other nodes in the network. Eigenvector
centrality is based on the principle that importance of a node in a network may increase with
having links to the other nodes that are themselves important. It is calculated by providing a
relative score for each node proportional to the sum of the scores of its neighbours (Bonacich,
2007). The equations used to calculate the node centrality are given in Appendix 1.

are standardized to “supply chain management ” on the basis of the popularity.
