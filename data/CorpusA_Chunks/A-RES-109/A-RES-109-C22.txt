Closeness centrality is associated with a node ’s autonomy or independence in the whole
network. A node with higher closeness centrality indicates that it is less reliant on other
nodes and has a higher capacity for independent actions. From the results, we found that the
works of {89} Freidberg et al. (2011), {17} Ilbery et al. (2006a), {39} Kottila et al. (2008) and
{65} Fischer et al. (2009) have a high closeness centrality value, indicating the extensivity of
their influence in the whole network. A node with high betweenness centrality specifies that
it links various other nodes in the research domain, indicating that they play a more
important role in the whole network than in the focal cluster. For example, the works of {37}
Risku Norja et al. (2008), {89} Freidberg et al. (2011), {119} Reardon et al. (2012) and {41}
Leat et al. (2008) have a high betweenness centrality value and serve as a bridge in the whole
network connecting different clusters. From Table VI, it is clear that the articles from
Clusters 2 and 3 have a higher betweenness centrality as compared to Cluster 4, implying
that the research concepts of Cluster 4 have less association with other clusters.
Eigenvector centrality considers whether a node has many neighbours or important
neighbours or both. From Table VI, higher eigenvector centrality is observed for the articles
in Cluster 2, followed by {27} Croom et al. (2007), {39} Kottila et al. (2008) and {65} Fischer
et al. (2009) of Cluster 3. Cluster 4 has the least eigenvector centrality in which the lead
articles have an eigenvector centrality of 0.0442, indicating that the research issue of these
clusters has less influence in the network. To identify the research theme in each cluster, we
further conducted a TM technique and established a co-word network as shown in Figure 4.
After establishing the co-word network, the research concept of each cluster is then
identified by measuring the network properties based on degree, closeness, betweenness
and eigenvector centralities (Milojevic, 2013).
The top 10 words in each cluster are identified by measuring the relative frequency of
co-occurrence and centralities as presented in Table VII. The degree measure reported in
Table VII shows that market economics of contract farming in SME FSC, alternative food
network (AFN), supply chain relationship in the food industry and land use and emission
impact of livestock systems are the research concepts of Clusters 1, 2, 3 and 4, respectively.
It is observed from Table VII that the keywords Supply, Chain, Farmer, Food, Market and
System are found in more than one cluster. These are bridging keywords that highlight the
association between the clusters.
It is also observed that Clusters 1 and 2 have more bridge keywords with high
betweenness centrality, indicating their close association with other clusters. In Cluster 4,
only one bridge keyword is identified from among the top 10 keywords, indicating that this
sub-field has less association with the other clusters. It is worth noting that this bridge
keyword has a high eigenvector centrality in few clusters and low eigenvector centrality in
other clusters, indicating its importance within the clusters. The keyword “SME” appeared
in Clusters 1 and 2 within the top 10, indicating its strong association with both the clusters,
whereas it appeared at the 17th position with low centralities in Cluster 3, indicating its
weak association with the theme of Cluster 3. It is also observed that the word “SME” did
not appear in Cluster 4 during this period.
3.2.1 Bibliographic network during 2002 – 2018. To trace the dynamic evolution of the
research domain, we repeated the analysis after adding the research articles published from
2015 to 2018. Since the data are almost doubled, we limited to at least seven couplings with
minimum of two other articles resulting in the number of included articles to 228 (see
Appendix 3). By applying the default Louvain algorithm in Gephi, the 228 bibliographic
coupled articles are grouped into five clusters as shown in Figure 5. The modularity index of
the network is 0.429, indicating a strong association between the nodes within and between
the clusters.
