A. Fruits and Vegetables 
India is the second largest producer in Fruit and 
vegetable processing Sector. The annual 
horticultural production is 100 million metric 
hectares i.e., over 18% of India’s gross 
agricultural output and has been grown on 12 
million hectares which means 7% of the 
country’s total cropped area. Fruits and 
vegetables are equally divided between the 
organized and unorganized sectors, with the holding of 48% of the share in organized sector.

B. Meat and Poultry 
India has a livestock population of 470 million, 
which includes 205 million cattle and 90 million 
buffaloes or we can say, 50% of buffaloes and 
1/6th of the goat population. The Processing of 
meat products is licensed under Meat Food 
Products Order, (MFPO), 1973. Only about 1% -
2% of the total meat is converted into value 
added products. The Food and agriculture 
organization estimates 4.42 million tonn es production of meat and poultry products. The 
population culled for meat are 11% buffalo, 6% Cattle, 33% sheep and 38% goat. Most of the 
meat are purchased raw and freshly consumed at home, restaurants and vice versa. Poultry 
processing is also at a nasc ent stage. The country produces about 450 million broilers and 33 
billion eggs annually. The growth rate of egg and broiler production is 16% and 20% 
respectively. In the case of poultry, export from India is mostly to Maldives and Oman. Other 
markets such as Japan, Malaysia, Indonesia, and Singapore are being explored. The growing 
number of fast food outlets in the country has had a significant impact on the meat processing 
industry in India. As per capita incomes rise and urban families live in smaller un its, the 
demand for processed meat products, which can be quickly cooked, has been rising. Most of 
the production of meat and meat products continues to be in the unorganised sector. Some 
branded products like Venky’s and Godrej’s Real Chicken are, however, becoming popular in 
the domestic market.
