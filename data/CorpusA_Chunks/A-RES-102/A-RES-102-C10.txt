In the digitally transforming agri-food sector, securing 
data is vital, especially with rising geopolitical tensions 
heightening cyber threats. Robust cybersecurity is cru -
cial to protect sensitive data and critical infrastructure 
against these threats [37] . Blockchain technology is 
particularly beneficial, enhancing traceability and redu -
cing fraud in cross-border e-commerce and food supply 
chains [38] . Keeping hardware and software updated is 
essential for cybersecurity. With increased awareness 
and stricter data protection regulations, including man -
datory breach notifications and higher fines, the sector is 
expected to align its digital transformation with these 
cybersecurity demands. Companies need to navigate 
these regulations while ensuring data security and 
privacy compliance. However, failing to adopt cyberse -
curity practices to the agri-food sector’s unique structure 
and complexities could lead to vulnerabilities, risking 
food supply chain disruptions. As reliance on DT grows 
in agriculture, cybersecurity risks underscore the need 
for significant investment in cybersecurity research and 
development to safeguard the food and feed supply.
Challenges of digital transformation in agri- 
food systems
One of the primary barriers to successful digital trans -
formation is the concern over data privacy and security. 
Both public and private organizations regularly en -
counter difficulties in guaranteeing the security and 
privacy of data shared during digital transformation in -
itiatives [39] . This can result in a lack of trust among 
stakeholders and impede collaboration. Establishing 
trust among stakeholders is essential for the effective 
implementation of digital transformation [40] . Issues 
such as a lack of transparency in data exchange processes 
and governance can create obstacles to collaboration and 
impede progress. Additionally, power dynamics within 
supply chains can pose a significant barrier to successful 
digital transformation efforts [41] . Companies may en -
counter resistance from suppliers or partners who are 
reluctant to embrace new technologies or processes.
The digital divide is yet another obstacle to transfor -
mation, referring to the gap between those with access to 
DTs and those without [42] . This discrepancy can 
hinder digital transformation, as organizations may 
struggle to engage with stakeholders who lack digital 
literacy or essential technological infrastructure. Ad -
ditionally, inadequate technology infrastructure can im -
pede digital transformation efforts, especially in regions 
with limited connectivity or outdated systems.
Organizations may face challenges implementing new 
technologies or processes without the necessary infra -
structure in place [43] . Integrating various systems and 
technologies can also be complex, particularly when sys -
tems are not compatible. This lack of interoperability can 
create barriers to data exchange and collaboration be -
tween organizations. Inconsistent data formats and stan -
dards can further impede the exchange of information, 
resulting in inefficiencies and errors. Achieving data in -
teroperability is crucial for successful digital transforma -
tion initiatives [44] . Organizations operating across 
borders must navigate a complex regulatory environment, 
characterized by varying data protection and privacy laws 
in different countries [45] . A deeper engagement with 
governance and policy aspects, including interoperability, 
regulations, and compliance, is obvious for ensuring effi -
ciency, legal certainty, and sustainable growth.
The digitalization of the agri-food system faces key aca -
demic challenges. These include designing IT systems with 
a clear purpose, specifying accurate data requirements, uti -
lizing big data analytics, developing digital twins, and en -
suring data interoperability and standardization. Moreover, 
there is a lack of reference and ground truth data for vali -
dating sensor data and enhancing AI capabilities in agri -
culture. The nature of diversified and circular agri-food 
systems further complicates these challenges, requiring in -
novative and adaptable digital solutions.
Digitalization requires an interdisciplinary approach, 
combining expertise from data science, computer sci -
ence, system engineering (including AI, Machine 
Learning, and Big Data), and communication technolo -
gies. Additionally, it demands insights from social sci -
ences, economics, business, and organizational studies to 
understand its broader impacts. Knowledge in produc -
tion and operations management, along with biological 
science and domain-specific expertise, is crucial. 
Importantly, digitalization must be evaluated at a system 
level, not just at the device level, to ensure holistic and 
sustainable integration across various domains.
Digital transformation of agri-food systems requires a 
systemic approach to digitalization that looks beyond in -
dividual technologies to explore interconnected techno -
logical systems, such as IoT and sensor networks, and 
data ecosystems, as well as how they interact with agri -
cultural, ecological, economic, and regulatory systems. 
This requires integration across user-level digitalization, 
farm-level digitalization, agri-food chain digitalization, 
and policy-level digitalization. This multilevel integration
